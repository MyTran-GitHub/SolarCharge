Exactly how the calculations work

Per hour: Grid import = max(0, home + EV − PV). Export = max(0, PV − home − EV).

Modeled variable net bill: sum(import × import rate) − sum(export × export credit).

Charging optimizer: minimizes additional modeled bill cost across feasible hours using an hourly dynamic program at 0.25 kWh resolution, subject to charger power, vehicle availability and ready-by battery energy. It can choose solar, grid energy, or a mix.

Comparison: Savings = overnight-plan EV-attributable cost − selected-plan EV-attributable cost. No-EV home charges are held constant. The baseline itself must be feasible.

Not included: actual CCA export-generation tariff, full NBT credit banking/true-up, non-bypassable charges, taxes, site-level demand charges, grid carbon forecast, device control, real solar/weather feeds, or measured customer charging behavior. These can alter true bills.

Where do these numbers come from?

Verified reference: PG&E 2026-interconnection-vintage export-credit sheet (12 months × 24 hours × two day types); the PG&E delivery export component is used as published. E-ELEC peak-period boundaries are published. Assumptions: SVCE generation export, effective bundled import prices, solar production, home usage, and EV data are editable example inputs. This is not a quoted utility bill.

PG&E's price sheet explicitly states its “Energy Produced” component is for bundled PG&E generation service, not automatically SVCE CCA customers. Read SVCE Solar Billing Plan and PG&E E-ELEC periods.
